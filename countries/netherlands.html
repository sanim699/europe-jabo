Studying in the Netherlands

The Netherlands is known for high-quality, English-taught education and a practical, international-student-friendly system — though tuition runs higher than many other EU destinations.

Tuition & scholarships

Non-EU tuition typically ranges from roughly €8,000 to €20,000/year depending on the university and program, with research universities generally pricier than universities of applied sciences. The Holland Scholarship, funded by the Dutch government and participating institutions, offers a one-time award to help non-EU students with costs; individual universities also run their own scholarship schemes.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter (the university typically applies for your entry visa on your behalf)
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in the Netherlands
  • Health insurance valid for the Netherlands
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • MVV (long-stay entry visa) plus residence permit — most Dutch universities handle this application process jointly with you

Application timeline

  • Oct-Dec: Most applications open for the following September intake
  • Jan-Apr: Common application deadlines (many competitive programs close earlier — January is common for non-EU applicants)
  • Apr-Jun: Admission decisions and scholarship results
  • Jun-Aug: University begins MVV/visa process with you; gather supporting documents
  • Sep: Academic year begins

Language & exam requirements

Most English-taught programs require IELTS 6.5 or higher, with some competitive Master's programs asking for 7.0. Dutch-taught programs require Dutch language proficiency, though these are far less common for international students. Some Master's programs also require GMAT/GRE scores, particularly in business fields.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

Non-EU students in the Netherlands are generally allowed to work part-time during studies (commonly cited limits are around 16 hours/week during term, or full-time during summer months) under a work permit their employer typically arranges. After graduation, the Netherlands offers an "orientation year" residence permit allowing graduates to search for work or start a business — a notably generous post-study option.

Cost of living

Amsterdam is significantly more expensive than other Dutch cities. A typical monthly budget covering rent, food, and transport is roughly €900-€1,300/month in Amsterdam, and somewhat lower — around €700-€1,000/month — in cities like Groningen or Enschede.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with the Dutch embassy in Dhaka or your target university before applying.
