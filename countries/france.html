Studying in France

Public universities in France remain among the most affordable in Western Europe, and the number of English-taught Master's programs continues to grow, especially in business and engineering.

Tuition & scholarships

Public universities in France charge relatively low official tuition fees for international students at Bachelor's and Master's level compared to many other Western European countries, though "Grandes Écoles" and specialized institutions can charge considerably more. The French government and individual institutions offer scholarships such as Eiffel Excellence scholarships for Master's and PhD students, which are competitive but well regarded.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in France
  • Health insurance valid for France
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Completion of the Campus France procedure (required for most applicants from Bangladesh before applying for the visa)
  • Long-stay student visa (VLS-TS) application at the French consulate in Dhaka

Application timeline

  • Oct-Jan: Register with Campus France and begin program applications for the following September intake
  • Jan-Apr: Common application deadline window for many universities
  • Apr-Jun: Admission decisions arrive
  • Jun-Aug: Finalize Campus France procedure, gather visa documents, book appointment
  • Sep: Academic year begins

Language & exam requirements

For French-taught programs, proficiency around B2 level (often shown via DELF/DALF/TCF) is generally required. English-taught Master's programs typically ask for IELTS 6.5 or equivalent. Some business schools also request GMAT or GRE scores as part of the application.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students in France are generally permitted to work part-time during studies, commonly cited at up to around 964 hours per year (roughly equivalent to 60% of full-time work), though this should be confirmed against current rules. After completing a Master's degree, France offers a temporary residence permit allowing graduates to search for qualifying employment or start a business.

Cost of living

Paris is considerably more expensive than the rest of France. A typical monthly budget covering rent, food, and transport is roughly €1,000-€1,400/month in Paris, and around €700-€1,000/month in other university cities like Lyon, Toulouse, or Lille.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with Campus France or the French consulate in Dhaka before applying.
