Studying in Estonia

Estonia is known as one of Europe's most digitally advanced countries, with a growing number of English-taught programs in IT, engineering, and business at competitive costs.

Tuition & scholarships

Tuition varies by university and program, generally ranging from around €1,600 to €7,500/year for international students, with IT and technology programs often priced toward the lower end. Some Estonian universities offer partial tuition-waiver scholarships based on academic performance during the first year of study.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Estonia
  • Health insurance valid for Estonia/Schengen area
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Long-stay (type D) student visa application at the nearest accredited Estonian consulate
  • After arrival: apply for a temporary residence permit for study if staying longer than the visa period

Cost of living & part-time work

A typical monthly budget in Tallinn covering rent, food, and transport is roughly €600-€900/month, with smaller towns like Tartu somewhat cheaper. International students in Estonia are generally permitted to work part-time alongside their studies, which can help offset living costs — but always confirm current work-hour limits with your residence permit conditions.

Application timeline

  • Nov-Jan: Most bachelor's/master's applications open for autumn intake — Estonian universities generally have earlier deadlines than many EU peers
  • Jan-Mar: Typical application deadlines for many programs (check individual university pages, as this varies)
  • Mar-May: Admission decisions and scholarship announcements
  • Jun-Aug: Visa document preparation and appointment booking
  • Sep: Semester begins

Language & exam requirements

English-taught programs typically require IELTS 6.0-6.5, with some technical/IT programs at leading universities requesting slightly higher scores or additional motivation letters and sometimes an entrance test or interview, particularly for competitive computer science programs.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

Estonia is generally considered one of the more flexible EU countries for international student work rights, allowing part-time work during studies under residence permit conditions. Estonia's strong tech sector also means graduates in IT-related fields often find local pathways to employment-based residence permits — but confirm current hour limits and post-study visa options directly with Estonian authorities, as policies evolve.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with the Estonian consulate or your target university before applying.
