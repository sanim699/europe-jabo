Studying in Poland

Poland has become a major destination for international medicine programs and offers a wide range of affordable English-taught degrees across other fields too.

Tuition & scholarships

Tuition for international students ranges roughly €2,000-€5,000/year for most Bachelor's and Master's programs, while English-taught medicine programs are priced considerably higher, often €10,000-€14,000/year. Some Polish universities and the Polish government offer scholarships or partial tuition waivers for well-performing international students, though availability varies by institution.

Visa & residence permit checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Poland
  • Health insurance valid for Poland
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • National (type D) student visa application at the Polish embassy in Dhaka
  • After arrival: apply for a temporary residence permit (karta pobytu) if your program is longer than the visa period

Application timeline

  • Nov-Feb: Applications open for many programs for autumn intake — medicine programs often have earlier, more competitive deadlines
  • Mar-Jun: Common application deadline window (varies by university)
  • Apr-Jul: Admission decisions announced
  • Jul-Sep: Visa document preparation and embassy appointment booking
  • Oct: Semester begins

Language & exam requirements

English-taught programs typically require IELTS 6.0-6.5 or equivalent. Medicine programs almost always require a separate entrance exam covering biology and chemistry, and sometimes an interview — requirements vary meaningfully between medical universities, so check each one directly.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students in Poland are generally permitted to work part-time during studies once they hold a valid residence document, subject to hour limits. Graduates can apply for a temporary residence permit tied to employment if they secure a qualifying job offer — confirm current rules with Polish immigration authorities before relying on them.

Cost of living

Poland is one of the more affordable EU countries to live in. A typical monthly budget covering rent, food, and transport is roughly €450-€650/month, with Warsaw somewhat higher than smaller cities like Poznań or Lublin.
