Studying in Romania

Romania offers some of the lowest tuition fees in the EU, with a growing number of English-taught bachelor's and master's programs, especially in medicine, engineering, and business.

Tuition & scholarships

Public university tuition for international students in Romania is often among the most affordable in the EU, commonly in the range of €2,000-€5,000/year depending on the field (medicine tends to be higher). The Romanian government also offers scholarships for students from developing countries, including Bangladesh, covering tuition and providing a modest monthly allowance — competition exists but acceptance rates are generally more accessible than in Western Europe.

Visa & residence permit checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Romania
  • Health insurance valid for Romania
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Long-stay (type D) student visa application at the Romanian embassy in Dhaka
  • After arrival: register for a residence permit with Romanian immigration authorities within the required timeframe

Cost of living

Romania is one of the more budget-friendly EU countries to live in. Outside Bucharest, students can often manage on roughly €350-€500/month covering rent, food, and transport; Bucharest itself runs somewhat higher.

Application timeline

  • Nov-Feb: Romanian government scholarship applications for developing countries typically open — check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs portal
  • Mar-May: Direct university applications open for many programs
  • May-Jul: Admission and scholarship decisions arrive
  • Jul-Aug: Visa document preparation and embassy appointment booking
  • Sep-Oct: Semester begins (some programs start in October rather than September)

Language & exam requirements

English-taught programs generally require IELTS 6.0 or equivalent, though thresholds vary by university and field. Medicine programs often require a separate entrance exam or interview, sometimes covering biology and chemistry basics — check your specific university's admission page, as this varies more between Romanian universities than in some other EU countries.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students in Romania are generally permitted to work part-time alongside studies once their residence permit is issued, subject to hour limits. Graduates can explore employment-based residence permits if they secure a job offer after finishing their degree — as always, confirm current hour limits and post-study rules with Romanian immigration authorities before relying on them.
