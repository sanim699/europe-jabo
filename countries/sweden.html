Studying in Sweden

Sweden is known for high-quality, research-driven education and a strong culture of independent study — tuition is higher for non-EU students, but scholarships can offset this significantly.

Tuition & scholarships

Non-EU tuition typically ranges from roughly €8,000 to €20,000/year depending on the university and program. The Swedish Institute (SI) Scholarship is the most prominent funding option for international Master's students, covering tuition, living costs, and travel — it's highly competitive, so a strong, well-prepared application matters.

Visa & residence permit checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Sweden
  • Health insurance valid for Sweden (often included automatically once a residence permit is granted)
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Residence permit for studies application through the Swedish Migration Agency (this replaces a separate visa for stays over 90 days)

Application timeline

  • Oct-Jan: Applications open on the national Swedish university admissions portal for the following autumn intake
  • Jan: Main application deadline for most Bachelor's and Master's programs (mid-January is typical — this is an early, strict deadline)
  • Mar-Apr: Admission notifications and SI Scholarship results
  • Apr-Jun: Residence permit application and document preparation
  • Aug-Sep: Semester begins

Language & exam requirements

Nearly all international programs in Sweden are taught in English and typically require IELTS 6.5 or equivalent. Swedish language ability is not usually required for English-taught programs, though it helps with part-time work and daily life.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

Sweden does not impose a fixed weekly hour limit on part-time work for students holding a residence permit for studies, though your studies must remain your main activity. After graduation, Sweden offers a residence permit allowing graduates a period to search for work or start a business — check current permit duration with the Swedish Migration Agency.

Cost of living

Stockholm is the most expensive Swedish city for students. A typical monthly budget covering rent, food, and transport is roughly €900-€1,200/month in Stockholm, and somewhat lower — around €700-€950/month — in cities like Lund or Uppsala.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with the Swedish Migration Agency or your target university before applying.
