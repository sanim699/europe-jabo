Studying in Germany

Germany is one of the most popular destinations for Bangladeshi students worldwide, largely because most public universities charge little to no tuition, even for international students.

Tuition & scholarships

Most German public universities charge no tuition fee for Bachelor's and Master's programs, only a small semester contribution (often €150-€350) that usually includes a public transport pass. One notable exception is the state of Baden-Württemberg, which charges non-EU students a separate tuition fee. The DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service) is the main scholarship body for international students, offering funding for Master's and PhD study — competitive, but well worth applying to.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Blocked account (Sperrkonto) proof — a set amount must be deposited to show you can cover living costs for the year
  • Proof of accommodation in Germany
  • Health insurance valid for Germany
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • National (type D) student visa application at the German Mission in Dhaka

Application timeline

  • Apr-May: Research programs; German public universities often have earlier deadlines than other EU countries
  • May-Jul: Application deadlines for winter (October) intake at many universities
  • Jul-Sep: Admission decisions arrive; set up blocked account and gather visa documents
  • Sep-Oct: Visa appointment and travel
  • Oct: Winter semester begins (a smaller summer semester intake also exists at many universities, starting in April)

Language & exam requirements

English-taught programs typically require IELTS 6.0-6.5 or equivalent. If you're applying to a German-taught program, you'll generally need to show German proficiency around B2-C1 level, often via the TestDaF or DSH exam. Some universities and courses may also require a Studienkolleg (foundation year) if your Bangladeshi qualification isn't considered directly equivalent for university entry — check this carefully with your target university.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students in Germany are generally permitted to work a limited number of days per year alongside studies (commonly discussed as around 140 full days or 280 half days annually, though always confirm current limits). After graduation, Germany offers an 18-month residence permit to search for qualifying employment — one of the more generous post-study options in the EU, though rules can change.

Cost of living

Munich and Frankfurt are among the most expensive German cities; smaller university towns are considerably cheaper. A typical monthly budget covering rent, food, and transport ranges roughly €700-€1,100/month depending on the city.
