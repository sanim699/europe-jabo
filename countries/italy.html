Studying in Italy

Italy is popular with Bangladeshi students for its low public-university tuition and generous regional (DSU) scholarships. Here's what to actually expect.

Tuition & scholarships

Public university tuition in Italy is income-based and often very low — sometimes under €200/year for students who qualify. The DSU (Diritto allo Studio) regional scholarship covers tuition plus a living stipend for eligible students, but it is region-specific, so requirements vary by which region your university is in.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Italy
  • Health insurance valid for Italy/Schengen area
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Visa application form + appointment at the Italian embassy in Dhaka

Cost of living

Costs vary a lot by city — Milan and Rome are considerably more expensive than smaller university towns. Budget roughly €600-€900/month for a smaller city and €900-€1,300/month for Milan or Rome, covering rent, food, and transport.

Application timeline

  • Aug-Oct: Research programs, check IELTS/language requirements, start collecting documents
  • Nov-Jan: Most bachelor's/master's applications open for the following September intake
  • Feb-Apr: DSU regional scholarship applications typically open — deadlines vary by region, check early
  • May-Jul: Receive admission decisions, begin visa document preparation
  • Jul-Aug: Book visa appointment at the Italian embassy in Dhaka — appointment slots fill up fast in peak season
  • Sep: Semester begins

Language & exam requirements

For English-taught programs, most Italian universities require IELTS 6.0-6.5 (or equivalent), though this varies by university and level. Some universities also accept a university-administered English test in place of IELTS. If you're applying to an Italian-taught program, you'll need to demonstrate Italian proficiency, typically B1-B2 level. A few competitive programs (medicine, some engineering) require an entrance exam such as IMAT for medicine.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students on a student visa in Italy are generally permitted to work part-time (commonly up to around 20 hours/week during term). After graduation, Italy allows graduates to apply for a residence permit to search for work for a limited period — always confirm current hour limits and post-study visa rules directly with the embassy, since these details do change.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with the Italian embassy in Dhaka or your target university before applying.
