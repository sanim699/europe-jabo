Studying in Lithuania

Lithuania offers affordable tuition and living costs, a growing English-taught course catalog, and Vilnius consistently ranks as one of the more student-friendly capitals in the EU.

Tuition & scholarships

Tuition for international students at Lithuanian universities is generally moderate compared to Western Europe, often in the range of €1,800-€6,000/year depending on the program, with fields like medicine priced higher. Some universities and the Lithuanian government offer merit-based scholarships and tuition discounts for well-performing international students.

Visa document checklist

  • University acceptance/enrollment letter
  • Valid passport (6+ months beyond intended stay)
  • Proof of financial means (bank statement or scholarship letter)
  • Proof of accommodation in Lithuania
  • Health insurance valid for Lithuania/Schengen area
  • Academic transcripts and certificates (translated/attested if required)
  • Long-stay (type D) national visa application at the nearest accredited Lithuanian consulate
  • After arrival: apply for a temporary residence permit (leidimas gyventi) if your program is longer than one year

Cost of living

Vilnius is one of the more affordable EU capitals for students, with a typical monthly budget of roughly €500-€750/month covering rent, food, and transport. Smaller cities like Kaunas can be even more budget-friendly.

Application timeline

  • Nov-Feb: Applications open for many bachelor's/master's programs for autumn intake
  • Mar-Jun: Typical application deadlines (varies by university — some rolling admissions exist)
  • Apr-Jul: Admission decisions and scholarship announcements
  • Jul-Aug: Visa document preparation and embassy appointment booking
  • Sep: Semester begins

Language & exam requirements

Most English-taught programs require IELTS 6.0-6.5 or equivalent. Medicine and a few specialized programs may require additional entrance exams or interviews — check directly with the specific university, since Lithuanian universities vary in how they screen applicants for competitive fields.

Part-time work & staying after graduation

International students in Lithuania can generally work part-time during their studies once they hold a valid residence permit, subject to hour limits. After graduation, there are routes to apply for an employment-based residence permit if you secure a qualifying job offer — confirm current hour limits and post-study rules with Lithuanian immigration authorities, as these are subject to change.

This guide shares general information only. Always confirm current requirements with the Lithuanian consulate or your target university before applying.
